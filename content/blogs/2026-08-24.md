Ralph Epperson’s The New World Order (published 1990 by Publius Press; based on research completed around 1989) is a conspiracy-oriented book that interprets the reverse of the Great Seal of the United States and its appearance on the U.S. dollar bill as encoding a long-term plan for a global totalitarian system. 


Epperson, an American researcher of conspiracy theories, argued that he spent roughly 27 years studying the Seal’s symbols (the unfinished pyramid, the all seeing eye, and the Latin motto Novus Ordo Seclorum). He translates the motto as “New World Order” (or a close variant announcing its birth) and claims the designers linked by him to Freemasonic influence among early American figures committed the United States to a hidden “Secret Destiny.” Because that destiny was unpleasant, he says, it was concealed in esoteric symbols rather than stated openly. 


Core Thesis: According to Epperson, a continuous network of secret societies (tracing back thousands of years through ancient “Mysteries,” and more recently including the Bavarian Illuminati founded by Adam Weishaupt in 1776 and elements of Freemasonry) has worked to establish a single world government, a unified world religion, and a collectivist economic system.

Key elements of this alleged New World Order include:
Abolition or severe restriction of national sovereignty and borders in favor of global governance.
Elimination of private property (redistribution from “have” to “have-not” nations/peoples, with the state controlling production).
Destruction or radical redefinition of the traditional family (state rearing of children, easier divorce, legalization of practices he opposed, reduced role for traditional homemaking).
Replacement of traditional (especially Christian) religion with a humanistic or Luciferian system in which reason is elevated, man can become god-like through hidden knowledge (gnosis), and Lucifer is portrayed positively as the “Light-Bearer.”
Use of situation ethics (ends justify the means) that can rationalize large-scale death or upheaval as necessary for the transition.
Population reduction targeting resisters (often framed by him in religious terms). 


He connects this agenda to the New Age movement (citing figures such as Alice Bailey and predictions involving a world teacher called Lord Maitreya expected around the year 2000), high-degree Freemasonry (especially the 33rd degree and writings of Albert Pike), communism (portraying Karl Marx as aligned with Satanic rather than purely atheistic aims), aspects of Nazism under Hitler, and statements by modern political figures about a “new world order.” 


### Structure and Approach

The book examines symbolism (serpents, stars, suns, pyramids, obelisks), secret-society history and rituals, attacks on religion/family/education/property/nationalism, and a claimed timeline culminating near 2000. Epperson presents extensive quotations from primary and secondary sources he believes support the thesis, while writing as a historian rather than purely as a religious polemicist (though he is a Christian and occasionally references the Bible). He ends with a call for awareness and corrective action by the public. 


Later notes by the author acknowledged that the specific 1 January 2000 start date he anticipated did not occur, while maintaining that the underlying plan continued on a revised, secret schedule.ContextThis is fringe literature within the broader genre of Illuminati/Freemasonry/New World Order conspiracy theories popular in certain circles in the late 20th century. Mainstream historians treat the Great Seal’s motto as referring to the founding of a new American era (drawing on classical sources), view the symbols as Enlightenment-era or neoclassical rather than a coded Luciferian program, and reject the continuous ancient conspiracy narrative as unsupported by rigorous evidence. Epperson’s interpretations of sources (including Masonic texts and historical figures) are selective and contested.The book functions primarily as a warning and interpretive guide to symbols and historical events through the lens of a planned global transformation directed by secret societies.

### Books from His Bibliographies and Citations (Further Reading He Draws Upon)

In The Unseen Hand and The New World Order, Epperson includes selected bibliographies of sources he researched and effectively recommends for deeper study. These appear in discussions of his work and related YouTube comments/contexts. Key examples include:

The Naked Capitalist by W. Cleon Skousen (summary/review of Quigley).
