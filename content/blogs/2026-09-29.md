A. Ralph Epperson's *The New World Order* came out from Publius Press in Tucson, copyright 1990. The book runs about 350 pages, with notes and an index. It is not a history of foreign policy. It is an argument that the reverse of the Great Seal of the United States is a confession.

His earlier book, *The Unseen Hand*, had already laid out a conspiratorial reading of American history. This one zooms in on the pictures. The unfinished pyramid, the eye, the eagle, the Latin. Epperson says the men who designed those two circles committed the country to a secret destiny, and that they hid it because people would have rejected the plan if it had been written in plain English. He says he spent twenty-seven years on the two sides of the seal. The book was finished in 1989.

![Reverse of the one-dollar bill, with both sides of the Great Seal](/images/nwo/dollar-bill-reverse.jpg)

## What he says the motto means

The whole book hangs on one translation. Under the pyramid are the words *Novus Ordo Seclorum*. Epperson renders that as "the New World Order." On the back cover, the longer line *Annuit Coeptis Novus Ordo Seclorum* is treated as "announcing the birth of the New World Order."

That is not a minor slip he corrects later. It is the key he announces on the cover, and every later chapter turns in it. If the motto does not mean what he says it means, the seal is no longer a confession. It is a national emblem with a paper trail, and the rest of the book has to borrow its drama from somewhere else.

In the introduction he tells the reader how the change would arrive. Not by a vote that names itself. By a crisis. A famine, a depression, a war. He thought it might begin after 1989, and that the structure would be in place by 1999. The later chapters come back to that date. Hold it. The date is the one part of the book that can be checked without a Latin dictionary, and it failed in public.

## How the book is built

He does not start with Congress in 1782. He starts with a mood. Something is wrong in America. Then he walks the reader through a single tunnel and calls every side passage the same tunnel.

The early chapters are religious and occult. Tomorrow's rulers. The New Age movement. Lord Maitreya, the world teacher that Benjamin Creme's circle was advertising in the 1980s. Ancient mysteries. Secret societies. Concealed mysteries. Serpents, stars, and suns. Then a short chapter of clarification, and then Lucifer worship, "becoming a god," and "sons of light." East and west. The Pyramid of Giza. Obelisks.

Only then does he reach the Illuminati, and from there the modern villains he prefers: Karl Marx, called a Satanist, and Adolf Hitler, called a Satanist. The Great Seal gets its own chapter late, after the theology. Then the Freemasons, the people who objected to his reading, Albert Pike, the legend of Hiram Abiff, a hierarchy, Masonic obligations, and the 33rd degree.

The last third leaves the symbols and files American life under the same plot. Secular humanism. Situation ethics. What he calls attacks on religion, the family, education, private property, and the nation. A chapter sets Russian law beside those changes, so the reader will see one author behind both. Then he answers the skeptic, reads Ronald Reagan and George H. W. Bush, and titles a chapter "Eleven Short Years." Summation. Solution. Questions.

Forty chapters, one cause. That is the method. A public argument, a monument, a lodge, a statute, and a newspaper item are not allowed to have their own reasons. If they can be placed on a line that begins at the pyramid, they go on the line.

## What the Latin says

*Novus ordo seclorum* means "a new order of the ages." Not "a new world order."

Charles Thomson, secretary of the Continental Congress, wrote the mottos when Congress adopted the seal on June 20, 1782. He adapted the phrase from Virgil. In the fourth *Eclogue* the line is *magnus ab integro saeclorum nascitur ordo*: a great order of the ages is born anew. Thomson's version marks a new age beginning with the republic. *Seclorum* is the genitive of *saeculum*, an age or generation. It is not the word for "world," and it is not a name for a world government.

*Annuit coeptis*, on the ribbon above the eye, is "He has favored our undertakings." The "He" is Providence. It does not mean "announcing." Thomson was a serious reader of Latin and of the Bible. He later published his own translation of the Septuagint. He was not hiding a modern political slogan inside a case ending.

![Reverse of the Great Seal: the unfinished pyramid and the Eye of Providence](/images/nwo/great-seal-reverse.png)

The phrase "new world order" does exist in English. H. G. Wells used it as a book title in 1940, arguing in the open for a postwar federation. George H. W. Bush used it in a speech to Congress on September 11, 1990, about the hope that nations would act together after the Cold War. Those are public sentences in English. They are not a translation of Thomson's motto, and they are not a leak. Epperson's book and Bush's speech landed in the same year. The book treats the speech as confirmation. A man describing a war policy in front of Congress is doing the opposite of confessing a secret carved in 1782.

Once the translation is wrong, the "secret destiny" has to be smuggled in from somewhere else. The seal stops being evidence and becomes a peg.

## How the seal was actually drawn

The seal was not delivered by a hidden lodge. It was a six-year argument in Congress, and the drafts survived.

On July 4, 1776, Congress asked Benjamin Franklin, John Adams, and Thomas Jefferson for a device. They brought in Pierre Eugène du Simitière, a Swiss artist in Philadelphia. Their report, read on August 20 and then tabled, already contained pieces that lasted. A shield. The motto *E Pluribus Unum*. The Eye of Providence in a radiant triangle. The date MDCCLXXVI. Franklin's proposal for the reverse was biblical and blunt: Moses at the Red Sea, with the motto "Rebellion to tyrants is obedience to God." Jefferson favored the children of Israel in the wilderness. Nobody in that room was encoding a Bavarian student society that had been founded ten weeks earlier, in a university town those men were not corresponding with.

Congress ignored the report for years. A second committee sat in 1780: James Lovell, John Morin Scott, and William Churchill Houston, with Francis Hopkinson doing most of the design. Hopkinson, who had worked on Continental currency, offered a shield of thirteen stripes, an olive branch, and a constellation of thirteen stars. For a reverse he used a pyramid. He had already drawn a pyramid of thirteen steps on the 1778 fifty-dollar note. Congress did not approve this design either.

In May 1782 a third committee, Middleton, Rutledge, and Elias Boudinot, hired William Barton, a young Philadelphian who knew heraldry. Barton moved fast. His reverse was an unfinished pyramid of thirteen steps with the eye above it. He had first thought of a palm tree on the summit, then crossed it out. His mottos were not Epperson's. One of them was *Deo Favente*, "with God favoring." Thomson later replaced it because it sat too close to mottos already in use.

On June 13, 1782, Congress handed the pile to Thomson. He took the eagle, which Barton had used, and made it the center. Olive branch and arrows for the power of peace and war. The first committee's motto in the beak. Thirteen stars from the second committee. For the reverse he kept Barton's unfinished pyramid, put the first committee's eye in a triangle at the zenith, restored the date 1776, and wrote *Annuit Coeptis* and *Novus Ordo Seclorum* himself. Congress approved the report the same day. The first die was cut that autumn and used to seal the peace.

That is the chain of custody. Three committees, named consultants, a secretary of Congress, a vote, a die. The occult reading needs this process to be a disguise. The papers show a committee that could not settle, and a secretary who finished the job in a week.

## The eye and the pyramid

The eye in a triangle is the Eye of Providence. It is a Christian picture of God watching, common in European religious art long before 1776. Du Simitière put it on a crest the way a herald would, as glory over a shield. The Bavarian Illuminati did not use it. Their emblem was the Owl of Minerva.

The pyramid has thirteen courses because there were thirteen states. It is unfinished because the country was unfinished. Thomson said as much in his remarks to Congress: the pyramid signifies strength and duration, and the eye over it alludes to the many interventions of Providence in favor of the American cause. He did not say it signifies a future world state. He explained the device, in English, in the same report that adopted it. A secret that is explained in the adopting resolution is not a secret.

The Roman numerals at the base, MDCCLXXVI, are 1776. That is the year of the Declaration. It is also the year Adam Weishaupt, a professor of canon law at Ingolstadt, founded the Illuminati, on May 1. Epperson reads the shared year as a signature. The first committee was dating a declaration it had just signed. Weishaupt was recruiting a handful of students in Bavaria. The designers of the seal were not dating him.

![The Eye of Providence](/images/nwo/eye-of-providence.png)

The other side of the seal is the one the early republic actually used in wax. A bald eagle, a shield with thirteen stripes, an olive branch of thirteen leaves and thirteen olives, a bundle of thirteen arrows, thirteen stars, and *E Pluribus Unum*: out of many, one. Count the thirteens. They are the states. One republic made of those states. Not one world government. The reverse was barely struck. For a century and a half most Americans who knew the seal knew the eagle.

![Obverse of the Great Seal](/images/nwo/great-seal-obverse.png)

## Why the reverse is on the dollar

The pyramid side landed on the back of the one-dollar bill in 1935. Henry Wallace, then Secretary of Agriculture, suggested it. Franklin Roosevelt approved it. Wallace had a taste for mystical systems, and conspiracy writing treats his suggestion as the moment the secret was finally allowed into everyone's pocket.

Look at what changed and what did not. No new symbol was added. No new motto. The Treasury printed a design Congress had adopted in 1782 and then neglected. A mystic in the cabinet can prefer an old engraving without thereby receiving instructions from Weishaupt. The bill is also covered in things Epperson's thesis does not need and does not explain: the word ONE, the Treasury seal, "In God We Trust," which was not on the original seal at all. If the dollar were a coded announcement, it would be an announcement that also says, in large letters, that it is one dollar.

## The Illuminati, and the end of them

Weishaupt's order was real. That is what makes the later myth possible. He founded it on May 1, 1776, inside a Catholic university, and he modeled the discipline partly on the Jesuits he had come to hate. Members took classical names. They were to advance by degrees, to prefer reason to superstition, and to work their way into Masonic lodges and into posts in government. At its height the order had on the order of a couple of thousand members, concentrated in German-speaking Europe. It was a faction of the Enlightenment with a taste for secrecy. It was not a state.

The Bavarian elector, Charles Theodore, banned unauthorized secret societies in 1784 and named the Illuminati in a further edict in 1785. Raids produced membership lists and papers. Weishaupt fled to Gotha and lived there, writing, until his death in 1830. The organization did not survive as a body that could steer a revolution in France, write a constitution in Philadelphia, or staff a central bank in 1913.

The myth that it did survive is also datable. In 1797 John Robison published *Proofs of a Conspiracy* and the Abbé Augustin Barruel began his memoirs of Jacobinism. Both blamed the French Revolution on a philosophic plot and gave the Illuminati a starring role. Those books were answered in their own time, and the historiography of 1789 has not adopted them. France had a bankrupt crown, a bad harvest, a war debt from the American Revolution, and a political class that had stopped believing in the old order. That is a sufficient cause. A suppressed lodge in Ingolstadt is not.

Epperson's method is to deny that ending. If a later institution is influential, it is the same plot wearing a new name. Freemasonry, because some founders were Masons and because Weishaupt had tried to enter lodges. The Federal Reserve, because bankers met at Jekyll Island in November 1910 and Congress passed the Federal Reserve Act on December 23, 1913. The Council on Foreign Relations. The United Nations. Bush's sentence in 1990. None of these facts are hidden. The Jekyll Island meeting was elite and quiet, and it is fair to say so. It was also a draft that had to pass a legislature, and the institution it produced operates under a statute, publishes minutes of a kind, and can be reformed or abused in the open. A quiet drafting meeting is not a continuous secret society. Calling it the Illuminati saves the writer from having to describe a central bank.

## Giza is not the seal

Epperson gives the Great Pyramid its own chapter, and the pictures in this genre always put Giza next to the dollar, as if one were a quotation of the other.

![The pyramids at Giza](/images/nwo/giza-pyramids.jpg)

The Great Pyramid was built for Khufu in the Fourth Dynasty, around 2600 BC. It is a tomb and a machine for a royal afterlife, aligned to the cardinal points with astonishing care. In the nineteenth century a separate fad, pyramidology, claimed that the passages encoded later history in a "pyramid inch." John Taylor and Charles Piazzi Smyth were the famous English versions. Egyptologists do not use that system. The courses are not a calendar of modern politics.

The pyramid on the seal has thirteen steps because Hopkinson and Barton were counting colonies. It is a neoclassical emblem of duration, drawn by men who had seen the form on currency and in books of symbols. It is not a measured elevation of Khufu's monument, and it does not contain his chambers. Putting the two pictures side by side is an argument by resemblance. Resemblance is the whole of it.

## Obelisks

The obelisk gets the same treatment. Conspiracy writing points at the Washington Monument, at the obelisk in St. Peter's Square, and at Cleopatra's Needle in London, and calls them markers left by one cult. Three stones, three cities, one author.

![An Egyptian obelisk](/images/nwo/obelisk.jpg)

An obelisk is an Egyptian solar monument, usually raised at a temple. Rome took them as trophies. The one now in St. Peter's Square was moved there in 1586 under Sixtus V, by the engineer Domenico Fontana. It had already been in Rome since antiquity. London's Cleopatra's Needle was erected on the Thames in 1878. Its pair went to Central Park in 1881. Those were diplomatic gifts and imperial display, the same decades in which European and American cities filled themselves with Egyptian style. A fashion is not a placement ritual.

![The Washington Monument](/images/nwo/washington-monument.jpg)

The Washington Monument is a memorial to George Washington. Robert Mills designed it. The cornerstone was laid on July 4, 1848. Work stopped for lack of money, for the Know-Nothing fight over the stone, and for the Civil War. The capstone was set on December 6, 1884, and the monument was dedicated the next February. It is about 555 feet of stone. The interruption is in the masonry, visible as a change in color, because the quarry and the money changed. A cult that can keep a plan secret for centuries had a strange amount of trouble finishing one obelisk on the Mall.

## Freemasonry, Pike, and the 33rd degree

The Masonic chapters are the long middle of the book, and they depend on a confusion the lodge itself encourages. Masonry is full of secrecy. The modes of recognition are secret. The ritual is allegory and is not explained to outsiders. Epperson treats that ordinary fraternal secrecy as political secrecy. They are not the same thing. A password that keeps non-members out of a meeting is not a policy that keeps citizens out of the statute book.

![Square and compasses, the emblem of Freemasonry](/images/nwo/square-and-compasses.png)

Some founders were Masons. Washington was. Franklin was. Many were not, or were Masons the way they were vestrymen, as a fact about their social life rather than the authorship of the Constitution. A fraternal lodge is not a legislature. The convention of 1787 kept its own secrecy, for the usual political reason, and then published a constitution and spent a year defending it in the newspapers. *The Federalist* is a strange document for a cabal that prefers symbols.

Hiram Abiff, whom Epperson gives a chapter, is the architect of Solomon's temple in Masonic ritual. He is murdered for refusing to give up the master's word, and the candidate is identified with him. It is a play about fidelity and death, built out of older legends. There is no historical biography of Hiram Abiff to decode into a coup. Reading it as a plan is like reading a passion play as the minutes of a committee.

Albert Pike is the other name. He was a Confederate general, a lawyer, and a Scottish Rite Mason. In 1871 he published *Morals and Dogma*, a long, public commentary on the degrees. Conspiracy writers lift his discussion of the name Lucifer and treat it as the creed of every lodge. Pike was wandering through comparative myth in a book anyone could buy. He was not issuing a secret order that blue-lodge members recite. A great deal of the "Masons worship Lucifer" shelf does not even come from Pike. It comes from Léo Taxil, a French anticlerical who in the 1890s invented a Luciferian "Palladian" Masonry and a witness named Diana Vaughan, sold the story to Catholics eager to believe it, and then, in 1897, announced that it had been a hoax. The hoax outlived the confession.

The supposed letter from Pike to Giuseppe Mazzini, laying out three world wars in advance, belongs in the same drawer. It was popularized in the 1950s by William Guy Carr, who claimed it sat in the British Museum. The Museum has no such letter. A prediction that cannot be found is not a prediction. It is a plot summary written after two of the wars had already happened.

The 33rd degree is the honorary top of the Scottish Rite in the United States. It is an administrative honor for a small number of members. Most American Masons never take it. They are entered, passed, and raised in a local lodge, three degrees, and they stop. A degree that most Masons do not hold cannot be the initiation into a world government. If it were, the government would be staffed by a few hundred men whose names are printed in Scottish Rite proceedings.

## Marx and Hitler in the same plot

Chapters 16 and 17 call Karl Marx and Adolf Hitler Satanists. The evidence on Marx is a set of violent, gothic poems he wrote as a young man, which this genre reads as a religious confession. They read like what they are: a student in the 1830s trying on the blasphemy that was already a literary pose. The evidence on Hitler is that his regime was pagan at the edges, occult at the fringe, and murderous at the center. None of that makes him a colleague of Marx in a lodge.

The pairing is the tell. Marx and Hitler understood themselves as enemies. Their states fought until one of them was destroyed. A theory that needs both the *Communist Manifesto* and the Third Reich as tools of one secret society has to flatten that war into theater. Theater is available. It is not history. People who publish manifestos and people who start wars usually mean the thing they say they are doing. The harder and less exciting task is to describe those projects on their own terms. Nazism was not a disguise for communism. Communism was not a disguise for the Great Seal.

## The politics he files under one conspiracy

The last third of the book is a catalog of changes Epperson disliked in American life, each assigned to the plot.

Secular humanism was a real movement, with signed manifestos in 1933 and 1973. Situation ethics was a real book, Joseph Fletcher's, in 1966. Arguments over school prayer, sex education, no-fault divorce, and property were the ordinary fights of the 1960s and 1970s. The religious right of those years, Francis Schaeffer and Tim LaHaye among others, already described humanism as a rival religion. Epperson's addition is the author. He does not need one. The people who wanted those changes wrote books, won elections, lost elections, and signed their names. A published manifesto is the opposite of a concealed mystery.

Setting "Russian laws" beside American school policy is the same move at the scale of the Cold War. The Soviet Union had a constitution, a party, a police, and a doctrine. The United States had arguments in state legislatures. Similarity of a phrase, or of a desired reform, is not identity of command. If it were, every country that opened a public school would be a branch of the same lodge.

You can think those reforms were good or disastrous. That argument is available without the pyramid. The pyramid is what lets the writer skip the argument and go straight to a culprit who cannot show up and answer.

## The deadline

Chapter 37 is titled "Eleven Short Years." The arithmetic is his. Writing at the end of the 1980s, he takes remarks about a new world eleven years out, adds them to a news story about the Millennium Society booking the Great Pyramid for a party on New Year's Eve 1999, and concludes that the order would begin in 2000. He even folds George Bush into the party, as if a society booking a monument were a head of state inaugurating a reign.

A private group reserving a tourist site for a millennium party is a fact about tourism and publicity. It is not a liturgical calendar. Bush did not go to Giza to install a new age. The year 2000 arrived. The United States still had a constitution, a border, and an argument about both. In a note added to a later edition, Epperson conceded that the order had not begun on January 1, 2000, and said the miss did not touch the research. A missed date that does not count as evidence against the theory is not a date. It is an ornament. The theory has been arranged so that fulfillment proves it and failure also proves it, because failure can be renamed "one step at a time."

That is why the introduction's crisis list still circulates, long after 1999. Any famine, any crash, any war can be called the step he allowed for. A prediction that accepts every disaster as confirmation, and also accepts the absence of disaster as delay, cannot be wrong. It also cannot tell you anything.

Later posts on the internet have pasted this book's cover onto warnings about pandemics and technologies the text does not contain. The cover is real. Those insertions are not. A false deadline was enough. It does not need a second career as a prophecy of whatever is happening this year.

![The United Nations flag](/images/nwo/un-flag.png)

The United Nations flag, which this literature reads as a claim of ownership, is a world map seen from the North Pole, with olive branches. It was adopted in 1946 as the emblem of an organization the Allies built so the next war would have a table to fail at first. It does not dissolve the United States. It does not command an American army. A picture of the earth is not a title deed. The UN is a bureaucracy with a charter, a security council that deadlocks, and members who ignore it when it is inconvenient. That weakness is visible. It is a reason to argue about the institution. It is not a reason to decide that the flag is a seal.

## Where this story turns into a lie

Books in this family rarely stop at "elites meet and pursue their interests." They name a hidden people who are supposed to be running it. Often that people is Jews, routed through the Rothschilds or a vaguer "international banker." That story is the *Protocols of the Elders of Zion*, a forgery cooked up by the czarist police and exposed as a forgery within a few years, retold with American clip art.

Epperson's shelf is that shelf. *The Unseen Hand* had already sorted modern history into one conspiratorial hand. This book keeps the hand and gives it a set of pictures. Influential families and lobbying shops are real. A racial secret government is not. If a theory needs a people, rather than a set of institutions you can open a statute book and find, it is not history. It is a scapegoat with footnotes.

The test is simple. When the writer shows you a committee report, a date on a die, a membership list seized in Munich in the 1780s, you are in the archive. When the writer shows you a people, and then explains Marx, Hitler, the dollar, the school board, and the UN as that people's tools, you have left the archive. The pictures are still on the page. The cause has been swapped.

## What is left if you throw out the destiny

People with money and office coordinate. Central banks exist and can be argued with. The meeting at Jekyll Island happened, and the Federal Reserve is a fair target for criticism of its structure, its errors, and its politics. Foreign-policy clubs publish, recruit, and nudge. The Council on Foreign Relations is a club for people who already have access. That is worth saying in a plain voice. It does not require an owl, a pyramid inch, or a forged letter from Pike.

You can oppose a war, a bank, a treaty, or a change in schooling without believing the unfinished pyramid is a timetable. The opposition is stronger when it names the statute, the vote, and the interest. It gets weaker the moment it needs Weishaupt to still be in the room. He is not. He died in Gotha in 1830, and the order he founded had already been broken by the police of a German prince.

*The New World Order* is worth reading as a specimen of a method. Watch what it does with one Latin phrase. A motto about a new age becomes a world government. A shared year becomes a signature. A committee that published its report becomes a disguise. A monument becomes a marker. A millennium party becomes an inauguration. A speech becomes a confession. Each step is small. Together they build a destiny that was never on the seal, and a deadline the calendar has already retired.

The seal says something plainer. Thomson wrote it down. Thirteen states, one republic, strength and duration, and a hope that Providence had favored the attempt. That explanation went to Congress on the day Congress adopted the design. That is the whole secret.
