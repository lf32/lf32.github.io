George Orwell finished *Nineteen Eighty-Four* in 1948, on the island of Jura, sick with the tuberculosis that would kill him in January 1950. Secker & Warburg published it in London in June 1949. The working title had been *The Last Man in Europe*. The title it got is the year he was writing, turned around.

It is the book people hold up whenever a camera goes on a pole or a ministry chooses a soft word for a hard thing. Sometimes that gesture is fair. Often it is costume. The novel is narrower than its reputation, and worse.

## What the world is

The story sits in London, which is now a chief city of Oceania, one of three super-states. The others are Eurasia and Eastasia. They rotate as allies and enemies. The war never ends, and it is not meant to. Its use is to burn surplus production so the standard of living never rises far enough to threaten the hierarchy, and to keep the population in a fear that makes loyalty feel like sense.

Oceania is governed by the Party, which has an Inner Party and an Outer Party, and below them the proles, most of the population, left a lottery, a beer, and popular songs. The Party does not bother to watch the proles tightly. It has decided they will not become dangerous unless they become conscious, and it does not expect them to. Above the whole arrangement is Big Brother. He may not exist as a man. The posters do not require him to.

Winston Smith is an Outer Party man who works at the Ministry of Truth. His job is to rewrite back numbers of the *Times* so that the Party's current line has always been the line. A person who falls out of favor becomes an unperson. The articles that mentioned him are replaced. The originals go down a memory hole to be burned. Winston's rebellion, at the start, is small. He buys a blank book and writes a diary. In that world the diary is already a crime, because the Party claims the inside of the head.

He falls in with Julia, who is younger and more practical. She hates the Party as a set of rules against pleasure. He hates it as a lie about reality. They rent a room without a telescreen, which is the closest thing the book has to a private life. Then O'Brien, an Inner Party man who has seemed to be recruiting them for a secret resistance, turns out to be the resistance's opposite. The Brotherhood they thought they were joining is a trap, or a fiction, or both. Winston is arrested, and the rest of the book is what the Party does with a man who still believes that two and two are four.

## The Ministry of Truth

The telescreen is the image everyone remembers. It watches and it talks. It is not the invention the book depends on. A camera in a living room is a tool. The Ministry of Truth is the system.

Winston does not invent the news. He corrects the past until it agrees with whatever was decided this morning. If the chocolate ration went down, the back file is changed so that the Party promised the lower number and then over-delivered. If Oceania is at war with Eastasia, it has always been at war with Eastasia, and the records of the war with Eurasia were a hallucination the reader only thinks he remembers. Colleagues vanish and the minutes of their existence vanish with them. There is no archive to appeal to, because the archive is the department Winston works in.

This is the sentence the book is built on. Who controls the present controls the record. Who controls the record controls the past. Who controls the past controls what a person is allowed to mean by "what happened." Orwell had watched a smaller version of it. In Spain in 1937 he was in the POUM militia. He saw the street fighting in Barcelona, and then he saw newspapers, including papers on his own side's broader left, describe that fighting as a fascist rising that had not happened the way he had just watched it happen. He was wounded at the front, and then he and his wife had to get out of Spain because the POUM had been declared a traitor organization. *Homage to Catalonia* is the account. *Nineteen Eighty-Four* is what that experience looks like after a decade of thinking about it.

The lie does not have to convince a historian. It has to be the only copy. A man can keep a private doubt for a while. He cannot check it. The Party's bet is that an unchecked doubt dies, and that a man who must perform the new line in public will eventually need the performance to be true, because living in two minds on purpose is a kind of work most people will not do forever.

## Doublethink

Lying is the simple case. The liar knows the fact and says the other thing. Doublethink is the trained ability to hold both, to know you are holding both, and then to forget the moment you noticed. The Party member learns to forget on command, and to forget that he was commanded. Tomorrow he will use the new fact with a clear conscience. Yesterday's fact is not stored somewhere as a guilty secret. It has been made unavailable to him.

This is why the book is bored by the idea of a cynical elite that privately laughs. O'Brien is not a hypocrite in the ordinary sense. When he tells Winston that reality is inside the skull, and that the skull belongs to the Party, he means it. Power is not a means to comfort. In the theory the book puts in O'Brien's mouth, power is the point. The boot on the face is not an accident of the revolution. It is the picture of a successful one.

You can reject that theory as a description of every real dictatorship. Most of them are also theft. The secret police do well out of the job. Orwell knew that. His point is sharper than a satire of grafters. A system that only wants money can be bargained with, or waited out, or robbed in turn. A system that wants the contents of the mind has to break the last standard the mind uses when it is alone. Arithmetic is that standard. If the Party can make two and two into five, not as a password you repeat and privately cancel, but as a sum you accept, then there is no place left from which to call anything else a lie.

Winston understands this before he fails it. The freedom he wants, by the end, is not the vote and not Julia. It is the freedom to say that two and two make four. If that is granted, the rest follows. The Party agrees with him about the stakes, which is why it does not shoot him at the start.

## Room 101

Room 101 is the room where they keep the worst thing, particular to the prisoner. For Winston it is rats. The scene is short and it is enough. He does not negotiate a philosophy. He gives them Julia. Do it to her.

The Party is satisfied only when the betrayal is real. A signature would have been cheaper. What it requires is that the last loyalty inside him, the one that was not political but personal, be handed over. After that, the re-education is almost clerical. He sits in a café. He follows the war news. He cries over a victory. He loves Big Brother.

The ending is not a twist for its own sake. It is the demonstration. If the book stopped at the arrest, Winston would remain a tragic witness, and the reader could leave with a martyr. Orwell will not issue that comfort. The Party does not make corpses out of heretics when it can make believers. A corpse still implies that a heresy was possible. A believer implies that the heresy was a mistake the heretic now sees.

## Newspeak, and the appendix people skip

The slogans on the Ministry are the short form. War is peace. Freedom is slavery. Ignorance is strength. They look like paradoxes posted for effect. Inside the book they are policies. War is the peace of a fixed hierarchy. Freedom, as the Party defines it, is slavery to the Party, and is called freedom so that the other kind cannot be asked for under its own name. Ignorance is a duty of the member, not a lack.

Newspeak is the long form. The project is to shrink the language until thoughts the Party dislikes cannot be said, and then, in the next generation, cannot be had. A word like "free" survives only in sentences about a field being free of weeds. The political sense is not redefined. It is made missing. There are vocabularies for a Party member's daily work, for technical jobs, and for ideology, and the ideological list is a set of names for approved attitudes, with the understanding that the vocabulary will get smaller every year.

Orwell had already written the argument in plain English, in "Politics and the English Language" in 1946. Dying metaphors, ready-made phrases, abstract nouns standing where a verb and a fact should be. Political language, he said, is designed to make lies sound truthful and murder respectable, and to give an appearance of solidity to pure wind. Newspeak is that essay with the state as the editor. You do not have to ban a sentence if the parts required to build it have been withdrawn.

The appendix, "The Principles of Newspeak," is easy to skip because it is dry and because the story has already ended. It is written in ordinary English, in the past tense, as a description of a reform that was still being completed. Readers have taken that tense as a crack of light. If someone in a later century can describe Newspeak from outside, in the old language, then the project of making the old language unusable did not finish. The novel itself gives you no such ending. Winston loves Big Brother. Both documents are in the book Orwell published. The appendix does not cancel the last page. It does tell you that the language war was the war he thought mattered, enough to explain it once more after the plot was over.

## Where the book came from

Orwell was a socialist. He said so, and he meant the democratic kind. He had no patience for the idea that equality is a comfort you offer people instead of freedom. *Animal Farm*, four years earlier, is the same argument as a fable. The pigs do not start as monsters. They start as the animals who can read. The commandment changes one clause at a time. By the end the farmers and the pigs are indistinguishable, and the other animals cannot quite remember the wording they had painted on the barn. Publishers flinched. Gollancz refused it. Cape took it and then dropped it after a nudge from the Ministry of Information. Faber declined. It was 1944, the Soviet Union was an ally, and a fable about a revolution eaten by its leaders was treated as a breach of manners. That episode is a small Ministry of Truth of its own, run by respectable men in a free country.

He had also spent two years at the BBC, from 1941 to 1943, producing cultural talks for broadcasts to India. He did the work and disliked the institution. The Ministry of Truth's canteen, its wasted effort, its cubicles, its clever people doing a dirty mechanical job, owe something to those years. Propaganda does not require a genius in every office. It requires enough educated people who will correct a text because correcting texts is the job.

When *Nineteen Eighty-Four* came out, some reviewers in America read it as an attack on the British Labour government, which had been in office since 1945 and was building a welfare state. Orwell moved to shut that reading down. He told his publisher it was not an attack on socialism or on Labour, which he supported. It was a warning about the perversions a centralized economy is liable to, already partly realized in Communism and in Fascism. He did not think Attlee was Big Brother. He thought a style of mind that had flourished in Moscow and Berlin could travel, including into parties that still called themselves liberators.

He had seen that style up close, and he had also seen the British left hesitate to describe it while the Soviet Union was doing the fighting against Hitler. The hesitation is one of the book's targets. Not the worker. The intellectual who can talk himself into a lie because the lie is on his side, and who then polices other people for remembering the previous version.

## What it is not

The book is not a prediction that the year 1984 would look like London in the novel. Orwell set it in a year that, when he wrote, was far enough off to be a warning and near enough to be about his own century. Treating it as a calendar is the same mistake Epperson makes with a motto. The year passed. The mechanism did not get disproved by the calendar.

It is also not a sticker you can put on any policy you dislike. A fine, a camera in a shop, a rude word discouraged by a newspaper, a rival party winning an election: none of these is the Ministry of Love. Oceania is a one-party state that tortures people until they surrender the plain evidence of their senses, and that burns the old newspaper so there is nothing to check. If you call every annoyance by that name, you use up the name. When a government does start altering the record and punishing people for remembering the unaltered one, the audience you trained will hear a slogan they have already decided is hysteria.

The opposite error is to file the book under Stalin and close it in 1991. The Soviet Union was the largest model in front of him, and Nazism was the other. The mechanism is older and cheaper than either regime. Own the file. Punish the private copy. Require the performance of belief, not only the silence. Do that long enough and you will not need to persuade. You will have people who defend the new line as if they had never heard the old one, because remembering it is expensive and, after a while, impossible to prove.

There is a third misreading, popular because it flatters the reader. Winston is not a hero who stays pure inside. The book is careful about his pettiness, his desire, his snobbery about the proles, his willingness to throw acid in a child's face if the Brotherhood asked, which O'Brien gets him to say before the arrest. The Party breaks a man who was already more ordinary than the poster of the book suggests. That is the frightening part. The last man in Europe was not built for the role.

## What is left

What survives the misuse is a test you can apply without a telescreen.

Can you look up what was said last year, in a copy the speaker does not control? If the only remaining copy is the one the speaker has edited, you are in Winston's department, whether or not anyone has built the camera.

Can you say that a number is the number it is, when a statement from authority says otherwise, and keep your place? If the penalty for the sum is not argument but disappearance, the arithmetic has stopped being yours.

Can you tell a private loyalty from a public one? The Party's victory condition is not the poster. It is the moment Winston wants the rats on Julia. A state, a church, a movement, or a company that trains people to offer up the person next to them, and calls that offering loyalty, is running the same exercise at whatever scale it can afford.

Orwell did not think the exercise was the whole future. He thought it was a habit of power, and that the counter-habit was plain language and a record. Say what happened. Keep a copy. Refuse the softened noun when the verb is "killed" or "lied." He practiced that in the essays, unevenly, like anyone. The novel is the account of a place where the practice has been made a crime.

The last page is bleak on purpose. He does not promise that the proles will wake up, though Winston hopes for it and the book does not confirm it. He does not promise that the appendix is a history written by the winners of a later revolt. He promises only that if you lose the distinction between what happened and what the present finds convenient, you will not get it back by being intelligent. Winston is intelligent. O'Brien is more intelligent. Intelligence is the instrument. It is not the obstacle.
